## 09_2026-10-21_Kateryna

**Що змінилось:** Загальний висновок про «weakest link» замінено на те, що ми змінили в підході. Додано питання. Бажано посилання на кейс.

### Чернетка системи
We built an AI tool that made doctors’ note-taking faster. After four iterations, we removed it.
We tested a few approaches, refined the feature across four iterations, then took it out entirely. Not because it stopped working. Technically, it did exactly what it was built to do.
Doctors simply found their own shorthand faster to write than reading through an AI-generated summary. The summaries tended to run long, and pulling the one piece of context they actually needed out of a longer text took more time than just writing it themselves in the first place.
A similar pattern shows up in scheduling. A booking system for a doctor’s calendar doesn’t live or die on the software. It lives or dies on whether the doctor actually manages their own schedule well to begin with.
Both point to the same thing. A system — AI included — is only as good as its weakest link, and the weakest link is almost always the person using it. Their habits and their actual needs decide how useful a tool ends up being, not how advanced the technology behind it is.
It’s the question worth asking before any AI project starts: not whether something can be automated, but whose habits it actually has to fit.
